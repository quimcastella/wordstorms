    Drop a few text files on the left, one per document: speeches, chapters, reviews, survey answers. Words they share keep the same place, color and angle in every cloud, so similar documents look similar and what's distinctive stands out.

    How it works

    1. Pick words. Each document's most frequent words, ignoring common English words like "the" and "and".
    2. Place shared words together. A word in several documents goes to the average of its positions, cloud by cloud, until positions settle.
    3. Refine. A force layout pulls each word toward its position in similar documents, pushes overlaps apart and keeps clouds compact.
    4. Fill in. Words unique to one document fill the space around them.

    Everything runs in your browser; your files never leave your computer. Method from Castella & Sutton, Word Storms: Multiples of Word Clouds for Visual Comparison of Documents (WWW 2014). Fonts are open-source, from Google Fonts via Fontsource.
