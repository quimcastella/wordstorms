    One cloud per document, laid out together

    A word storm is a group of word clouds. Words that appear in several documents get the same position, color and angle in every cloud, so you can compare documents at a glance: similar documents look similar, and what's distinctive stands out.

    Add a few text files on the left, or try the example.

    How it works

    1. Pick words. Each document's most frequent words, ignoring common English words like "the" and "and".
    2. Place shared words together. A word that appears in several documents is placed at the average of its positions, cloud by cloud, until positions settle.
    3. Refine. A force layout then pulls each shared word toward its position in similar documents, pushes overlapping words apart and keeps clouds compact.
    4. Fill in. Words unique to one document fill the space around them.

    Everything runs in your browser; your files never leave your computer. Method from Castella & Sutton, Word Storms: Multiples of Word Clouds for Visual Comparison of Documents (WWW 2014).
